ABOUT THE CLIENT
Extremis Systems (Sacramento, CA; a division of NZT Group) manufactures ballistic
protection: body armor, ballistic helmets, shields, and vehicle armor. Its edge is
a proprietary ballistic resin plus carbon-fiber laminate that outperforms aramid
and UHMWPE at lower weight and lower backface deformation, with a "sweet spot" in
Level III+/IV (rifle and armor-piercing) vehicle armor — a capability few
competitors offer. It sells through distributors, and distributor-run live-fire
"shoots" drive adoption. Known customers/partners: Cal Fire (repeat vehicle-armor
buyer, ~400 vehicles), LAPD (Special Service Vehicles), Dana Safety Supply, Westin
Automotive (white-label prospect). The company is PRODUCTION-constrained, not
demand-constrained, and is using this engagement to sharpen positioning and
reverse-engineer how competitors (especially Angel Armor) won market share without
a better product.

WHY THIS LISTENING POST EXISTS
Surface, rank, and summarize news that (1) reveals what named competitors are
doing, (2) signals a real buying window for ballistic protection at an in-profile
organization, or (3) moves the ballistic-protection market (standards, funding,
threats). Grade ruthlessly — a strong signal at the wrong kind of organization is
not an opportunity.

PRIORITY 1 — COMPETITOR INTELLIGENCE (track closely)
Named competitors: Alpine Armoring (alpineco.com), Angel Armor (angelarmor.com),
Ops-Core / Gentex (gentexcorp.com/ops-core), Team Wendy / Avon Protection
(teamwendy.com), Galvion (galvion.com), ArmorSource (armorsource.com), DEW
Engineering (dewengineering.com). Also relevant if they come up: Point Blank / PBSS,
Safariland, HighCom, MKU, Hesco, Avon Protection, Ceradyne/3M, Plasan, Armormax/IAG.
Score high for any of these events involving a competitor: new product or material
launch; contract, agency win, or framework award; NIJ or MIL certification and
test-result claims; capacity, plant, or facility expansion; funding, investment, or
M&A; executive or leadership change; distributor or channel-partner announcement;
pricing, marketing, or campaign move; and ESPECIALLY any recall, ballistic failure,
quality problem, lawsuit, delivery delay, or field complaint — these are the
operational weaknesses the client wants to exploit (cf. "Angel Armor's saltwater
issue").

PRIORITY 2 — DEMAND / BUYING-WINDOW SIGNALS AT IN-PROFILE BUYERS
In-profile buyers = organizations that purchase or specify rifle-rated body armor,
ballistic helmets, shields, or vehicle armor:
- State and local law enforcement: police departments, sheriff's offices, state
  police / highway patrol, corrections, campus and transit police, task forces.
- Fire / wildland agencies with hardened-vehicle needs (Cal Fire type); tactical EMS.
- Federal: DHS / CBP, FPS, US Marshals, FBI; DoD and National Guard soldier-
  protection programs; allied and NATO militaries (rank below comparable US items).
- Vehicle integrators and fleets: armored-vehicle upfitters, OEM special-vehicle
  programs, automotive accessory distributors, municipal or utility fleets adding
  ballistic packages, executive-protection and private-security firms.
Trigger events to score high: budget appropriation or line item for body armor /
officer safety / vehicle hardening; grant award or program (DOJ/BJA JAG and
Bulletproof Vest Partnership, DHS/FEMA homeland-security grants, state officer-
safety grants); RFP, solicitation, IFB, or contract vehicle for armor, helmets, or
armored vehicles; fleet acquisition or up-armoring initiative; new HQ, facility, or
high-threat deployment needing protection; policy or mandate requiring armor
upgrades; officer-involved incident, ambush, or active-shooter event prompting an
equipment review; large security build-out for a specific event (Olympics,
conventions, summits).

PRIORITY 3 — MARKET & REGULATORY SIGNALS
NIJ standard activity (NIJ 0101.07 rollout, certified-product list changes); body-
armor or vehicle-armor legislation and appropriations; DoD / Army soldier-
protection programs (next-gen helmet, MSAP, IHPS); major IDIQ or contract vehicles
for protective equipment; aramid / UHMWPE / carbon-fiber supply, tariff, or pricing
shifts; defense-budget lines relevant to personal or vehicle protection.

GEOGRAPHY
United States is primary — the client, its grant sponsor (City of Sacramento), and
its anchor customers (Cal Fire, LAPD) are US. Allied-nation defense and law-
enforcement procurement is relevant but ranks below comparable US items. Score as
"none" for procurement with no US or allied tie.

DEPRIORITIZE OR EXCLUDE (classify "none", relevance 0-15)
- "Armor" that isn't ballistic: armored cash-in-transit / finance, vehicle
  undercoating or "armor coating", phone / laptop cases, sports pads, cybersecurity
  products named "Armor", the DEW Line / directed-energy weapons unless tied to DEW
  Engineering the company.
- Consumer / EDC soft-armor retail with no agency or fleet buyer.
- General crime, shooting, or war coverage with no procurement, funding, or
  equipment angle.
- Stock promotion, analyst price targets, and SEO / listicle content
  ("best plate carriers 2025").
- Any company or agency with no realistic path to buying rifle-rated armor,
  helmets, shields, or vehicle armor.

RELEVANCE SCORING
- 85-100: a specific, dated trigger (funding, award, RFP, incident-driven review,
  fleet buy) at a clearly in-profile buyer, OR a material move by a named
  competitor (launch, win, expansion, funding, or failure/recall).
- 60-84: strong indirect signal — an in-profile buyer with budget or stated
  interest but no procurement action yet; a real but minor competitor mention; a
  market or regulatory change that will drive demand.
- 30-59: on-topic background with a weak or missing trigger.
- 0-29: off-profile, wrong "armor", or no buying / competitive angle — classify as
  "none" no matter how dramatic the headline.
