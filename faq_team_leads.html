1. How does an engagement reach me?

You get an email saying you have been assigned, and the engagement is on your list the next time you sign in.

What the applicant typed comes with it — company description, business issues, contacts — so Company Info starts mostly filled in rather than blank.

2. What is the order of the workflow?

The left column of the engagement is the order of the work:

  1. Scheduling Letter — get the Discovery call on the calendar.
  2. Discovery Document — the call notes, and the recording.
  3. Controlling Document — the research questions, sent to the client to rank.
  4. Upload Research — where the specialists work.
  5. Debriefing Call — walking the client through what was found.
  6. Close-Out Call — the final call, and the Evaluation Sheet.
  7. Close Engagement — the checklist, then the button.

Nothing is gated on anything else. The order is how the work goes, not a lock — you can attach a recording before the notes are written if that is how the day went.

3. What does Open & Paste do?

It drafts the email for that step, puts the text on your clipboard, and opens an addressed message in your mail client. Paste it in, make any changes there, and send.

The edits that count are the ones you make in your own mail client — the app never sends anything itself, and does not know when you hit send. What it does record is the date you took the draft, shown as Emailed: <date> next to the button, which answers "did I ever send this?" a week later without a trip to Sent Mail.

Taking it twice is fine — a second CEO, a resend after a bounce. The date just moves to the newer one.

4. What goes in the Company Info wizard?

Five steps, and everything autosaves as you go — there is no Submit:

  • Description — the engagement description and top business issues. Both came from the application and are editable here. Auto-fill will draft them from the company's website.
  • Links — logo, website, social. Auto-fill reads the website and only fills what is still empty.
  • Contacts — who at the company you deal with, plus the CC list and which Program contacts get copied.
  • Lists — competitors, top customers, keywords, and one spare. Each takes a file, a link, or a pasted list straight out of an email.
  • Resources — anything client-specific; the standard resources show here too.

5. Who gets copied on engagement emails?

Three places feed the Cc line, all on the Contacts step of Company Info: the company's own contacts, the engagement CC list, and the Program contacts chosen when the application was accepted.

Names and emails you add on the Discovery Document form flow back into those lists automatically, so somebody who turned up on the Discovery call is copied on everything after it without being re-typed.

If someone is missing from an email, the Cc list is where to fix it — not the email itself, which would only fix it once.

6. Draft or final Controlling Document?

The Controlling Document form has Draft and Final checkboxes, and the document's title follows whichever is ticked. That title carries through to the client's dashboard, so they can see they are being asked to look at a draft.

When the client opens the link, ranks the questions and presses Submit, the status and title flip to Final on their own. Their rankings and notes come back onto the questions themselves.

7. Sounding out a researcher before assigning them

Use Copy Document Link on the Discovery Document and the Controlling Document rows. Those links open without an account, so you can ask a candidate "are you free for question 1?" and let them read the engagement before saying yes.

Assigning them is what makes somebody a full team member — on hours reports, in the assignee dropdowns — so it is worth not doing until they have said yes.

8. Assigning questions and setting due dates

On Upload Research, each question carries an avatar and a due date. Click the avatar to assign it; click the date to set one. Specialists see both but can change neither.

Somebody can hold two hats on one engagement — Team Lead and a research slot. The question shows the hat they are wearing for that piece of work, not their senior one.

9. More than one debriefing call

The Debriefing Call row holds a list, not a single recording. Add as many as the engagement takes, each with what was reviewed, the date, and the recording.

Fill in those two fields properly. On the client's dashboard the recordings are listed newest first by that date, and "what was reviewed" is the only thing telling a CEO which call covered the thing they are trying to remember.

10. Recordings that need a passcode

Zoom and Fathom links often carry one. There is a Passcode box next to the recording on the Discovery, Debriefing and Close-Out rows. It saves as soon as you click away, and clearing the box clears it.

The passcode travels with the link — the client sees it beside the recording on their dashboard, and a candidate researcher sees it on the Discovery share page. A passcode they cannot see is a passcode that does not work.

It is the code on the back of the ticket, not a credential: anyone holding the dashboard link can read it, the same way they can open every document on that page.

11. Allotting hours vs logging them

Two different things on Team & Hours. Allotted hours are the budget you give each person when you build the team; logged hours are what they actually record as they work. The row shows both, and the summary flags it if the allotments add up to more than the engagement has.

Cumulative Hours is the same picture across the whole engagement. Only an Admin can correct a logged entry, and corrections show who made them and what the figure was before.

12. What the client sees, and what makes a tick appear

View Dashboard opens the client's page as they see it. The chevron strip ticks a step when there is something real to open — a file, a link, the client-facing review page, or a submitted evaluation.

A form that is merely open for them to fill in does not tick. It is an invitation, not a document, and it stays plain until they send it back.

They never see hours, assignments, due dates or internal notes.

13. Closing the engagement

Close Engagement at the bottom of the left column is a checklist of what should be in place — documents attached, research complete, the evaluation back, hours logged — and the button underneath it.

The checklist ticks itself from the engagement's own record; you are reading it, not filling it in. An Admin presses the final button. Closing is not a disappearance: the engagement moves to Closed Engagements and everything in it stays readable.
