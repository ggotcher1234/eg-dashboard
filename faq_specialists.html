1. Where are the engagements I am on?

Engagements in the top menu is the home page. The My Engagements tile counts the ones you are assigned to, and clicking it filters the list below to just those. The list has a Mine / All switch if you want to look wider.

Click any engagement to open its workspace. The left column is the whole engagement: Team & Hours, Company Info, the workflow steps, and View Dashboard.

A finished engagement does not disappear. Engagements → Closed Engagements opens past work, so last year's report is still there when you need to look something up.

2. How do I log my hours?

Open the engagement and go to Team & Hours. On your own row, click the number in the Spent Hours column — the number itself is the button, and it is underlined on the rows you are allowed to log against. The Log hours box opens; enter the number and submit. The entry is dated today — it is a log of work done, not a timesheet you fill in ahead.

Only your own rows are clickable. Everyone else's Spent Hours are plain text, which is the quickest way to find your row on a crowded engagement.

Hours are collected by the month you worked them. Whatever you log in a month is invoiced once that month closes and paid the month after, which the Log hours box tells you by name each time.

3. I logged the wrong hours. Can I fix it?

Not yourself — ask an Admin. They can correct both the figure and the date from the Billing History on that row.

Corrections are never silent. The entry keeps a line saying who corrected it, when, and what it was before, so if your total moves you can see why, and an invoice question months later still has an answer.

4. How do I see my Spent Hours?

The Billable Hours MTD tile on the home page is your own total for the current calendar month across every engagement. Click it to see the entries behind the number.

For one engagement, Team & Hours shows your Allotted Hours, your Spent Hours and what is Remaining, side by side on your row. Cumulative Hours shows the same for the whole team.

5. Where is the research I am meant to do?

In the engagement, open Upload Research. The research questions are listed there, each with the initials of whoever it is assigned to — yours are the ones with your initials.

Click a question to open it. Everything about that question is behind three pills:

  • Background — the context from the Discovery call and what the client said they wanted to know.
  • Summary — your two-sentence answer (see below).
  • Upload Research — the files or links themselves.

Due dates are set by the Team Lead. You can see yours on the row; you cannot change it, so if a date is wrong, say so rather than working to it.

6. What goes in the Summary?

Two sentences on what you found — the answer, not the method. The box stops at 280 characters and counts down, which is about two plain-English sentences.

It is short on purpose: it goes on the client's dashboard directly under their question, so a CEO reads your summary first and opens the documents only if they want the detail. If the finding genuinely needs more room, that is what the document is for.

7. How do I hand in the research itself?

Open the question, press the Upload Research pill, and add a file or paste a link. Either works, and you can add more than one.

What you attach appears on the client's dashboard under that question, in its research area. So name files like something a CEO will open — "Competitor pricing review" rather than "final_v3".

8. How do I mark a question finished?

Tick the checkbox at the left of the question row. That is what clears it off the Team Lead's list of outstanding work and stops the due date chasing you.

Tick it when the summary is written and the documents are attached — not when you have finished reading. Ticking it again un-ticks it, so a mistake costs nothing.

9. What does the client actually see?

View Dashboard in the left column opens the client's own page, exactly as they see it. Worth a look before you call anything finished.

They get a progress strip across the top — Discovery, Controlling Document, Debriefing, Close-Out — and below it the research laid out by area: each question, your summary, and the documents under it.

They do not see hours, assignments, due dates, or anything internal.
